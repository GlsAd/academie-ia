1. Why This Training Exists

The mission

AI is no longer a future topic — it is reshaping how work gets done today. But most employees encounter it through media noise or isolated tool experiments, without a coherent frame to understand what it means for their role, their team, and their organisation.

Agentic Academy was built to close that gap. In a single day, participants leave with:

  • A clear mental model of AI — from Predictive to Generative to Agentic — and why the shift matters
  • Practical confidence: they have used AI tools, written structured prompts, and built a personal AI assistant
  • An ethical compass: they can distinguish automation from augmentation, and name the traps to avoid
  • A shared vocabulary to use with colleagues when talking about AI at work

What success looks like

By the end of the day, each participant should be able to say:

  • "I understand the difference between Generative AI and Agentic AI"
  • "I can write a structured prompt using RACE and get a useful result"
  • "I have a personal AI assistant (Gem) configured for a recurring task"
  • "I know the four ethical considerations to keep in mind when deploying AI"
  • "I am less afraid and more curious than I was this morning"

4. General Facilitation Principles

Your posture

You are not a lecturer. You are a guide through a live experience. The activities are designed so that participants discover the key messages themselves — your role is to create the conditions for that discovery and name what just happened in the debrief.

  • Be curious, not prescriptive. When a participant says something surprising, explore it: "Tell me more about that."
  • Validate before correcting. Acknowledge the logic in someone's thinking before offering a reframe.
  • Use "we" not "you". "What we want to avoid is…" lands better than "You need to remember…"

Handling resistance to AI

ResistanceWhat they're really sayingSuggested response
"AI is just a fad"I don't want to invest in something that won't lastShow the three waves — AI is a 10-year evolution, not a trend
"I already use it every day"This might be too basic for meWelcome it: "Great — you'll be a resource for the group in the hands-on sections"
"Augmenting jobs = job losses"Real fearDon't dismiss it. "That's legitimate. Let's hold it for the Ethics round in the Card Game."
"I don't trust AI outputs"Healthy critical thinking"That's exactly the right instinct. Let's channel it into a workflow rather than an objection."

Managing mixed AI maturity in the room

The Profil IA Spider Graph gives you a live read of the room at 09:00. Use it actively:

  • If the room skews toward beginners: slow down Module 2, spend more time on the glossary, pair stronger users with beginners in the Card Game
  • If the room skews toward experienced users: move faster through the 3 waves, spend more time on the augmentation round and the ethics debate
  • Never make participants feel judged for their score. The Spider Graph is a starting point, not a ranking.

Pacing the group exercises

For the Card Game (Activity #3) and the RACE Builder (Activity #4), manage time actively:

  • Announce time checks at the halfway point and at 5 minutes remaining
  • If a group is stuck: ask "What's the simplest task in this scenario that AI could handle tonight, without anyone noticing?"
  • If a group is racing ahead: ask "Have you looked at the Ethics cards yet?"

The debrief is the learning

The 5–8 minutes of plenary debrief after each group activity is where the learning solidifies. Do not skip it even when you are short on time. Structure it as:

  1. What did you decide? (One spokesperson per group, 30 seconds each)
  2. What surprised you? (Open to the room)
  3. What does this tell us about working with AI? (You land the key message)

Why we run it

This activity does two things simultaneously: it breaks the ice by making the room's diversity visible without judgment, and it creates a personal baseline each participant will compare against at the end of the day. The Spider Graph becomes the day's red thread.

Key message

There is no right or wrong starting point. What matters is where you are by 15:15.

Main takeaways

  • I know my current AI profile across five dimensions
  • I can see how diverse the room is — AI maturity is not evenly distributed
  • I have a personal goal for the day

The five dimensions

DimensionScore 1 (low)Score 5 (high)
Fréquence (Usage)Never or almost neverMultiple times per day
Maîtrise (Prompting)Need help every timeGet what I want on the first try
Vigilance (Critical thinking)I trust it completelyI verify everything systematically
Protection (Vision)I know nothing about the risksI know and apply guardrails
Éthique & Sécurité"AI is a passing trend""It will change everything"

How to facilitate

  1. Ask everyone to open home.html and click the Icebreaker button (2 min)
  2. While they fill in the form, draw or display the Spider Graph axes on screen
  3. Once forms are in, ask for a show of hands per dimension per score — sketch the room's aggregate on screen (5 min)
  4. Open 5 minutes of discussion: "What surprised you in this room's profile?"
  5. Close with the key message and a forward pointer: "We'll run this again at the end of the day."

Talk-track

Facilitator script

"Look at the variety in this room. Some of you use AI daily, some of you have never opened ChatGPT. That's not a problem — it's actually why this format works. The people who've been using it have real experience to share. The people who haven't yet will bring questions that sharpen everyone's thinking. The Spider Graph you just filled in is yours — don't compare it to your neighbour's."

Tips & pitfalls

  • Participants may not know which score to give themselves — encourage them to go with their gut
  • If the room is mostly experienced users, spend less time on the group debrief and more on the "Why does the profile matter?" discussion
  • If someone scores themselves 5 on Vigilance: gently note that means they verify AI outputs every time — worth a quick warm "really?"

Time

Format

Why we run it

This module sets the conceptual frame for the entire day. Without it, the Card Game is just a task-sorting exercise. The four ethical considerations introduced here will be reactivated in Activity #3. The vocabulary (LLM, Agent, Hallucination) will be used in every subsequent activity.

Key message

The AI revolution is, above all, a human revolution. AI is a collaborator, not a replacement — the most important question is not "what can AI do?" but "what do we want humans to keep doing?"

Main takeaways

  • The three waves: Predictive → Generative (Copilots) → Agentic (Agents)
  • Generative AI predicts; Agentic AI acts — one answers, the other executes
  • Four ethical considerations: Bias, Data, Responsibility, Ecological impact
  • A shared vocabulary: LLM, Prompt, Hallucination, Agent, RAG…

Talk-track highlights

On the three waves

"Using historical data to make predictions has been around for a long time. More recently, Generative AI brought the notion of Copilots — assistive AI that augments how people work. Now, Salesforce is leading the way with the third wave: Agentic AI. Agents don't just assist. They take action. Use this with anyone who says AI is a new fad — it is actually a 10-year evolution."

The Benioff quote

"'We are the last generation to manage only humans.' — Marc Benioff, Salesforce CEO, World Economic Forum 2026. Think about that for a moment. In the near future, some of us will manage both humans and AI agents. The question is not whether AI will change work — it will. The question is which tasks we want to give it so we can focus on what matters most as humans."

On hallucination — use it to break the ice

"The word 'hallucination' always gets a reaction. AI can produce something that looks perfectly right and is completely wrong. It's not lying — it's predicting the most statistically probable output from its training data. That's why human judgment remains indispensable. This word alone is a good reason to always verify before you publish."

Tips & pitfalls

  • Do not skip the ethical considerations, even if short on time — a 5-minute mention is enough, they'll be fully unpacked in Activity #3
  • The glossary on module1.html is interactive — encourage participants to explore it themselves rather than reading every definition aloud
  • If someone says "I use it every day": welcome it, invite them to share a concrete example. It normalises practice and reassures hesitant participants
#3 AI Card Game — Maison Clarté Workshop 45 min · Morning
Time
10:30 – 11:15
Format
Group workshop — 3 rounds
Website
atelier.html
Groups
4–6 people per group

Why we run it

This is where theory becomes judgment. After Module 2, participants now apply the Automate/Augment/Ethics framework to a real-world case — Maison Clarté, a fictional retail chain. The three rounds are deliberately sequenced: automation is intuitive and fast; augmentation is harder and forces the "what is uniquely human?" question; ethics is the check that prevents over-deployment.

Key message

Automation is obvious. Augmentation is where the real value is — and it requires first answering the uncomfortable question: "What makes a human irreplaceable in this role?"

Main takeaways

  • Automation and augmentation are complementary levers with different risk profiles
  • The augmentation question is harder — and more strategically important — than automation
  • Every AI deployment has ethical implications; surface them before implementation, not after

The Maison Clarté case

Participants meet Julie, Head of Innovation at Maison Clarté (lifestyle retail: 20 boutiques + e-commerce). Three scenarios:

ScenarioUsersThe friction
A — "Les rapports…"Store managers (internal)20 weekly reports × ~1.5h = 30 human-hours/week lost. Only 30% read by HQ. Real alerts buried in noise.
B — "Les vendeurs…"In-store sales staff (internal)2,000-SKU catalogue impossible to memorise. Staff use personal phones to Google. Poor intranet.
C — "Le service client…"Customers + staff300 messages/week. 70% are 8 recurring questions. 3 agents drowning. 48h response time vs 4h target.

Running the three rounds

Split the room: Form groups of 4–6. Each group picks one scenario (or you assign them). Groups stay on the same scenario across all three rounds.

Round 1 — Automate (8 min)

Using the Automation cards on atelier.html, identify repetitive, low-value tasks that AI could handle. For each card selected, note: which card + what expected result.

Facilitator note

Groups fill this round in 5 minutes. That is expected. The speed itself is part of the lesson.

Round 2 — Augment (8 min)

Using the Augmentation cards, ask: how could AI make the people in this scenario better at what they do best? Think about empathy, judgment, strategy, relationship.

Critical facilitation note — let the silence happen

"This round always starts with a silence. Do not fill it. The question 'what makes a human irreplaceable in this role?' is not one people ask often. Let the discomfort exist. After 60–90 seconds, it unlocks a richer conversation than Round 1."

Round 3 — Ethics (6 min)

Given what you decided in Rounds 1 and 2 — are there risks you haven't considered? Who could be disadvantaged? Would you implement as-is, or adjust?

If a participant raises job displacement concerns in Round 2, answer: "That is a legitimate question. Hold it — it is exactly what Round 3 is for."

Plenary debrief (8 min)

  1. One spokesperson per group shares their top decision (30 seconds each)
  2. Open: "Which round was the hardest? Why?"
  3. Open: "Did any Ethics card change a decision you had already made?"
  4. Close: "What you just did is what most organisations skip. They jump to automation, skip augmentation, and discover ethical issues six months later. You just did it in 45 minutes."

Talk-track

Opening the Automate/Augment distinction

"When you look at your work and ask 'how can AI help?', there are two very different questions. The first: can AI do this for me? That's automation — and once you understand what AI can do, the use cases are obvious. The second is much harder: can AI make the person doing this work even better? That's augmentation. It requires first answering a question most organisations never really ask: what is the unique value of a human being in this role? Not what they do — what they are in that role."

Tips & pitfalls

  • If a group only picks automation cards: prompt them with "What happens to the store manager when she no longer spends 90 minutes on the report — what does she do with that time?"
  • If a group uses Ethics cards to veto everything: refocus — "Which of these risks could be mitigated by design?"
  • The flip cards work best when participants are on atelier.html on their own device
#4 Lab Prompting Expert — RACE Method 45 min · Morning
Time
11:30 – 12:15
Format
Individual hands-on (3 exercises)
Website
prompting.html
Materials
AI tool account required

Why we run it

This is the first time participants directly operate an AI tool in a structured way. The RACE method gives them a replicable framework they can use in their daily work immediately. The three exercises are sequenced from playful (Marc's retirement email) to professional — building confidence progressively.

Key message

Think of AI as an extremely capable junior who just joined the organisation. They know nothing about your context. The more precisely you brief them — with a role, an action, context, and requirements — the better the output.

Main takeaways

  • AI is a reasoning engine, not a search engine — you get out what you put in
  • The RACE framework produces reliably better results than unstructured requests
  • Iteration is the rule — the first result is a starting point, not a final answer
  • Never paste sensitive data into a consumer AI tool

The RACE framework

LetterQuestionExample
R — RôleWho should the AI be?"Act as an experienced project manager at [Company]"
A — ActionWhat exactly should it do?"Write an invitation email", "Summarise this text"
C — ContexteFor whom and why?"For Marc's retirement — 15 years, cycling fan, Friday 5pm auditorium"
E — ExigencesFormat, tone, constraints?"Humorous, max 3 paragraphs, include call to action for online collection"
Note for facilitators

The deck uses RTCF (Rôle, Tâche, Contexte, Format). The website and RACE Builder use RACE. Use RACE throughout — it matches the tool participants will use on screen.

Running the three exercises

Exercise 1 — Free prompt (5 min + 5 min debrief)

Ask participants to open their AI tool and write the Marc retirement email with no guidance. Debrief 2–3 results. Establish the "before" state.

Exercise 2 — Apply RACE (5 min + 2 min debrief)

Rewrite the same request using the RACE Builder on prompting.html. Compare outputs — what changed?

Exercise 3 — Professional use case (5 min + 5 min debrief)

Participants choose one of two cases from prompting.html:

  • Case A — Le Synthétiseur: Take a dense document; ask AI to simplify it for frontline staff and extract 3 concrete action items
  • Case B — L'Assistant de Réflexion: Ask AI to "ask me the right questions first before giving answers" — powerful for senior participants

The three reflexes to reinforce

  1. Critical mind: AI can hallucinate — always re-read before publishing or acting
  2. Data hygiene: Never paste sensitive data into a consumer AI tool
  3. Iteration: The first output is a draft. The real value comes from the conversation that follows.

Talk-track

Opening the activity

"A prompt is not a search query. When you Google something, you're looking for an existing document. When you prompt AI, you're commissioning something new. Think of AI as a brilliant junior who just walked in the door. They're fast, they're capable, they have no idea how your organisation works, and they will do exactly what you tell them — no more, no less. If your brief is vague, the output will be vague."

Tips & pitfalls

  • Some participants won't have an AI tool account — pair them with someone who does for Exercise 1
  • The Marc email always produces funny outputs — lean into it, laughter builds confidence
  • Case B (reflection assistant) is often the most valuable for senior participants — it shifts AI from writing tool to thinking partner
  • Watch for participants who skip RACE structure and write a long paragraph — gently redirect to the four-field structure
#5 Mon Premier Gem — Personal AI Assistant 45 min · Afternoon
Time
13:15 – 14:00
Format
Individual hands-on
Website
gemini.google.com (direct)
Materials
Google account required
Site note

This activity is in the deck but does not yet have a dedicated page on the training website. Direct participants to gemini.google.com. A gem.html page is planned for a future update.

Why we run it

Lab Prompting taught participants to write better one-off prompts. This activity takes the next step: a configured AI assistant that remembers its mission across every conversation. The Gem is the day's tangible take-away — participants leave with something they built themselves.

Key message

The shift from "writing a prompt every time" to "having an AI assistant that already knows its mission" is the shift from AI as a tool to AI as a collaborator.

Main takeaways

  • A Gem is a persistent AI assistant within Gemini, configured with a specific mission
  • Building a Gem requires the same thinking as a RACE prompt — just saved permanently
  • The right use case is any task you perform repeatedly (weekly reports, email drafting, meeting prep…)

How to run the activity

  1. Open discussion (3 min): "What is a Gem on Gemini?" — take 2–3 answers before explaining
  2. Demo your pre-built Gem (4 min): Show a Gem you built before the session. Demonstrate zero context-setting needed.
  3. Participants build their Gem (10 min): gemini.google.com → "My Gems" → "New Gem" → name, description, instructions
  4. Test and iterate (10 min): Each participant opens a new conversation with their Gem and runs a real task
  5. Debrief (8 min): 2–3 participants share their Gem concept and what they discovered

Suggested Gem concepts if participants are stuck

Gem nameUse caseStanding instruction (abbreviated)
Mon Assistant RéunionPrepare weekly team meeting"Act as a meeting facilitator. Generate a structured agenda with time blocks and expected outcomes."
Mon SynthétiseurCondense long documents"Extract 3 key ideas, 3 action items, 1 open question. Max 150 words, bullet form."
Mon Coach MailDraft professional emails"Draft in appropriate tone. Always offer two versions: formal and conversational."

Tips & pitfalls

  • Participants sometimes over-engineer their first Gem — encourage simplicity: "Start with one task you do every week"
  • The Gem instructions are essentially a saved RACE prompt — explicitly connect back to Activity #4
  • Some participants won't have a Google account — pair them or have them observe a neighbour's build
  • If time runs short, the "test" phase can be abbreviated — the important outcome is that the Gem exists and has been named

Why we run it

The Agentforce demo shows the qualitative leap from a copilot to an autonomous enterprise agent — one that handles 80% of a B2C customer service flow without human intervention. Key message

An AI Agent is not just a faster chatbot. It reasons, acts, remembers, and operates on trusted business data — autonomously, at scale, with humans at the helm for what matters most.

Main takeaways

  • Chatbot → Copilot → Agent: each step increases the level of autonomous action
  • Agentforce can handle 80% of routine cases — freeing agents for the 20% that truly needs them
  • Three pillars of a responsible Agentic Enterprise: Ethics by Design, Human at the Helm, Efficient Resource Use

Demo script (Agentforce B2C E-Commerce)

  1. Customer initiates contact → agent reads order history from Salesforce data
  2. Agent verifies return eligibility against policy
  3. Agent proposes options (exchange, refund, store credit) and processes the choice
  4. Agent logs the resolution and closes the case — zero human intervention
  5. Show the Agentforce Command Center — where human supervisors monitor and review escalations

Why we run it

This activity closes the loop opened at 09:00. Participants re-run the Spider Graph and compare it against their morning baseline. Even after one day, scores shift — particularly on Maîtrise and Vigilance. The comparison is the proof of learning.

Key message

AI literacy is not a destination — it is a practice. Today was day one. The Spider Graph shows you how far you can move in eight hours.

Main takeaways

  • I can see concrete progress between my morning and evening Spider Graph scores
  • I have a personal next step — one thing I will try differently next week
  • I know where to go to keep learning (module resource cards, Salesforce Trusted AI, Agentforce docs)

How to facilitate

  1. Ask everyone to return to home.html and click the Bilan button (2 min)
  2. Redisplay the Spider Graph template while they fill in the form
  3. Show-of-hands per dimension — sketch the evening aggregate next to the morning one (5 min)
  4. Ask: "What moved for you today? What surprised you about the shift?" (10 min)
  5. Each participant names: "One thing I'm doing differently next week" (5 min)
  6. Facilitator closing (5 min)

Closing talk-track

Facilitator closing script

"Look at where you started this morning and where you are now. Some of you told me at 09:00 that you'd never really used AI at work. You just spent a day understanding the technology, applying a prompting framework that professional engineers use, and building a personal AI assistant. That's not nothing. The Spider Graph doesn't lie — you moved.

AI is not a tool you learn once. It's a practice you develop over time — like managing people, or running meetings. The important thing is not to wait until you understand it perfectly before you start using it. Start with one real task. Use RACE. Iterate. And come back to the training site whenever you need to revisit a concept.

Thank you for the energy you brought today."

Tips & pitfalls

  • If time is short, the comparison can be done mentally — ask "On which dimension did you move most?" rather than redrawing the graph
  • Some participants will feel their scores "should" be higher — reassure them the Spider Graph is a tool for direction, not a performance metric
  • The "one thing next week" commitment is powerful — write them on a board or shared doc if possible

A. Key glossary

TermPlain-English definition
LLMLarge Language Model — the statistical engine behind tools like ChatGPT and Claude; trained on billions of documents
PromptThe instruction you give to an AI — the quality of the input directly determines the quality of the output
HallucinationWhen an AI generates something that looks correct but is factually wrong — it predicts plausibly, not truthfully
TokenThe unit of text AI processes — roughly 0.75 words; LLMs have a "context window" limit
CopilotAn AI assistant embedded in a workflow — helps, but doesn't act autonomously
Agent IAAn AI that receives a goal and chains autonomous actions: browse, write, send, book, decide
RAGRetrieval-Augmented Generation — connects an LLM to a specific knowledge base so it answers using your data
Fine-tuningRetraining a base LLM on a specific dataset to specialise its behaviour for a particular domain
Biais algorithmiqueWhen AI perpetuates or amplifies the biases present in its training data — subtle and easy to miss
Einstein Trust LayerSalesforce's data security architecture — your business data is never used to train external LLMs
Prompt injectionA security attack that tries to hijack an AI agent's behaviour by embedding hidden instructions in content it processes
GemA saved, persistent AI assistant in Gemini — configured with a standing mission, needs no re-briefing per conversation
AgentforceSalesforce's agentic AI platform — builds, deploys, and monitors AI agents that act on enterprise data
