## 1. Why This Training Exists

### The mission

AI is no longer a future topic — it is reshaping how work gets done today. But most employees encounter it through media noise or isolated tool experiments, without a coherent frame to understand what it means for their role, their team, and their organisation.

**Agentic Academy** was built to close that gap. In a single day, participants leave with:

- A clear mental model of AI — from Predictive to Generative to Agentic — and why the shift matters
- Practical confidence: they have used AI tools, written structured prompts, and built a personal AI assistant
- An ethical compass: they can distinguish automation from augmentation, and name the traps to avoid
- A vocabulary they share with colleagues, enabling more productive conversations about AI at work

### Partners and commitment

| Partner | Role |
|---|---|
| **Salesforce** | Training design, content, facilitation, Agentforce demo |
| **Fondation Simplon** | Pedagogical methodology, access, co-delivery |

The partnership reflects a shared conviction: AI literacy should not be reserved for tech teams. Every collaborator — regardless of role, seniority, or prior AI experience — deserves to understand and use AI with confidence and impact.

### Who is in the room

Participants are professionals from public and private organisations. The cohort will be mixed in terms of AI maturity. Expect a distribution roughly matching this persona framework:

| Persona | Profile | What they need from you |
|---|---|---|
| **Débutant curieux** | Low usage, open attitude | Reassurance that starting now is fine |
| **Hésitant informé** | Has heard of AI, hasn't tried it | Concrete, low-stakes first wins |
| **Praticien en chemin** | Occasional user, wants structure | A framework (RACE) to consolidate instincts |
| **Cow-boy** | Regular user, skips verification | The critical-thinking reframe |
| **Expert responsable** | High proficiency, aware of limits | Depth on ethics and augmentation |
| **Sceptique éclairé** | Informed but doubtful | Space to voice the tension without being dismissed |
| **Stratège observateur** | Strategic thinker, low personal use | The business case and the Benioff quote |

> You will see the actual distribution at 09:00 when participants complete the Baseline Profil IA. The Spider Graph makes the room's diversity visible — use it as a conversation starter, not a ranking.

### What success looks like

By the end of the day, each participant should be able to say:

- [ ] "I understand the difference between Generative AI and Agentic AI"
- [ ] "I can write a structured prompt using RACE and get a useful result"
- [ ] "I have a personal AI assistant (Gem) configured for a recurring task"
- [ ] "I know the four ethical considerations to keep in mind when deploying AI"
- [ ] "I am less afraid and more curious than I was this morning"

## 4. General Facilitation Principles

### Your posture

You are not a lecturer. You are a **guide through a live experience**. The activities are designed so that participants discover the key messages themselves — your role is to create the conditions for that discovery and name what just happened in the debrief.

- **Be curious, not prescriptive.** When a participant says something surprising, explore it: "Tell me more about that."
- **Validate before correcting.** If someone has a misconception, acknowledge the logic in their thinking before offering a reframe.
- **Use "we" not "you".** "What we want to avoid is…" lands better than "You need to remember…"

### Handling resistance to AI

You will encounter it. Common forms and suggested responses:

| Resistance | What they're really saying | Suggested response |
|---|---|---|
| "AI is just a fad" | "I don't want to invest in something that won't last" | Show the three waves slide — AI is a 10-year evolution, not a 2-year trend |
| "I already use it every day" | "This might be too basic for me" | Welcome it: "Great — you'll be a resource for the group in the hands-on sections" |
| "Augmenting jobs is just a polite way of saying job losses" | Real fear | Don't dismiss it. "That's a legitimate question. Let's hold it — the Ethics round in the Card Game is exactly where we'll unpack it." |
| "I don't trust AI outputs" | Healthy critical thinking | "That's exactly the right instinct. Let's channel it into a workflow rather than an objection." |

### Managing mixed AI maturity in the room

The Profil IA Spider Graph gives you a live read of the room at 09:00. Use it actively:

- If the room skews toward beginners: slow down Module 2, spend more time on the glossary, pair stronger users with beginners in the Card Game
- If the room skews toward experienced users: move faster through the 3 waves, spend more time on the augmentation round and the ethics debate
- **Never make participants feel judged for their score.** The Spider Graph is a starting point, not a ranking.

### Pacing the group exercises

For the Card Game (Activity #3) and the RACE Builder (Activity #4), manage time actively:

- Announce time checks at the halfway point and at 5 minutes remaining
- If a group is stuck: ask "What's the simplest task in this scenario that the AI could handle tonight, without anyone noticing?" — this unblocks the automation round immediately
- If a group is racing ahead: ask "Have you looked at the Ethics cards yet?" — this consistently slows the pace and deepens the conversation

### The debrief is the learning

The 5–8 minutes of plenary debrief after each group activity is where the learning solidifies. Do not skip it even when you are short on time. Structure it as:

1. **What did you decide?** (One spokesperson per group, 30 seconds each)
2. **What surprised you?** (Open to the room)
3. **What does this tell us about working with AI?** (You land the key message)

### Activity #1 — Baseline Profil IA · 30 min

**At a glance**

| | |
|---|---|
| Time | 09:00 – 09:30 |
| Format | Individual survey, then group share |
| Website | `home.html` → Icebreaker button |
| Materials | Participants' own devices; project the Spider Graph template on screen |

**Why we run it**

This activity does two things simultaneously: it **breaks the ice** by making the room's diversity visible without judgment, and it creates a **personal baseline** that each participant will compare against at the end of the day. The Spider Graph becomes the day's red thread — participants carry it mentally through every module.

**Key message**

> There is no right or wrong starting point. What matters is where you are by 15:15.

**Main takeaways**

- [ ] I know my current AI profile across five dimensions
- [ ] I can see how diverse the room is — AI maturity is not evenly distributed
- [ ] I have a personal goal for the day

**The five dimensions (Spider Graph)**

| Dimension | Scale low (1) | Scale high (5) |
|---|---|---|
| **Fréquence (Usage)** | Never or almost never | Multiple times per day |
| **Maîtrise (Prompting)** | Need help every time | Get what I want on the first try |
| **Vigilance (Critical thinking)** | I trust it completely | I verify everything systematically |
| **Protection (Vision)** | I know nothing about the risks | I know the risks and apply guardrails |
| **Éthique & Sécurité** | "AI is a passing trend" | "It will change everything" |

**How to facilitate**

1. Ask everyone to open `home.html` and click the Icebreaker button (2 min)
2. While they fill in the form, draw or display the Spider Graph axes on screen
3. Once forms are in, ask for a show of hands for each dimension at each score level — sketch the room's aggregate on the displayed graph (5 min)
4. Open 5 minutes of discussion: "What surprised you in this room's profile?"
5. Close with the key message and a forward pointer: "We'll run this again at the end of the day. Keep your score in mind."

**Talk-track highlight**

> "Look at the variety in this room. Some of you use AI daily, some of you have never opened ChatGPT. That's not a problem — it's actually why this format works. The people who've been using it have real experience to share. The people who haven't yet will bring questions that sharpen everyone's thinking. The Spider Graph you just filled in is yours — don't compare it to your neighbour's."

**Tips & pitfalls**

- Participants may not know which score to give themselves — encourage them to go with their gut, there are no wrong answers
- If the room is mostly experienced users, spend less time on the group debrief and more time on the "Why does the profile matter?" discussion
- If someone scores themselves 5 on everything: gently note that "Vigilance" scoring 5 means they verify AI outputs every time — worth a quick "really?" in a warm tone

### Activity #2 — Changement de Paradigme · 45 min

**At a glance**

| | |
|---|---|
| Time | 09:30 – 10:15 |
| Format | Plenary lecture with Q&A moments |
| Website | `module1.html` |
| Materials | Slides (deck sections 31–43), `module1.html` open on screen |

**Why we run it**

This module sets the **conceptual frame for the entire day**. Without it, the Card Game becomes a task-sorting exercise; with it, participants understand why Automate vs Augment is a meaningful distinction. The four ethical considerations introduced here will be reactivated in Activity #3. The vocabulary introduced (LLM, Agent, Hallucination, RACE) will be used in every subsequent activity.

**Key message**

> The AI revolution is, above all, a human revolution. AI is a collaborator, not a replacement — and the most important question is not "what can AI do?" but "what do we want humans to keep doing?"

**Main takeaways**

- [ ] The three waves of AI: Predictive → Generative (Copilots) → Agentic (Agents)
- [ ] Generative AI predicts; Agentic AI acts — one answers a question, the other executes a goal
- [ ] Four ethical considerations to always keep in mind: Bias, Data, Responsibility, Ecological impact
- [ ] A shared vocabulary to use throughout the day (LLM, Prompt, Hallucination, Agent, RAG…)

**Content map (use `module1.html` to guide pace)**

| Section | Key point | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Les 3 vagues de l'IA** | Wave 1: Predictive (lead scoring, fraud). Wave 2: Copilots (ChatGPT, Einstein GPT). Wave 3: Now — Agents. | 8 min |
| **IA Générative vs Agentique** | "Tool that thinks" vs "Tool that acts." Generative: you ask, it responds. Agentic: you give a goal, it chains actions. | 7 min |
| **La révolution humaine** | Benioff quote. Mollick / "Cybernetic Teammate." The question is not replacement but reallocation. | 10 min |
| **Les 4 enjeux éthiques** | Bias, Data, Responsibility, Ecological footprint. Brief — each will reappear in Activity #3. | 8 min |
| **Glossaire (interactive)** | Offer 5 min for participants to click through terms on `module1.html`. Do not read them all aloud. | 5 min |
| **Q&A** | Open floor. | 7 min |

**Talk-track highlights**

On the three waves:

> "Using historical data to make predictions has been around for a long time. More recently, Generative AI brought the notion of Copilots — assistive AI that augments how people work. Now, Salesforce is leading the way with the third wave: Agentic AI. Agents don't just assist. They take action. Use this with anyone who says AI is a new fad — it is actually a 10-year evolution they may have missed."

On Generative vs Agentic:

> "Generative AI: you ask a question, it answers. Agentic AI: you give an objective, it chains a sequence of actions — browse the web, send an email, book a meeting, trigger a workflow — without you having to intervene at each step. That is a qualitative leap, not just a speed improvement."

On the human revolution (the Benioff quote):

> "'We are the last generation to manage only humans.' — Marc Benioff, Salesforce CEO, World Economic Forum 2026. Think about that for a moment. In the near future, some of us will manage both humans and AI agents. The question is not whether AI will change work — it will. The question is which tasks we want to give it so we can focus on what matters most as humans."

On hallucination (use it to break the ice):

> "The word 'hallucination' always gets a reaction. AI can produce something that looks perfectly right and is completely wrong. It's not lying — it's predicting the most statistically probable output from its training data. That's why human judgment remains indispensable. This word alone is a good reason to always verify before you publish."

**Tips & pitfalls**

- **Do not skip the ethical considerations**, even if you are short on time. A 5-minute mention is enough — they will be fully unpacked in Activity #3
- **The glossary on `module1.html` is interactive** — encourage participants to explore it themselves rather than reading every definition aloud
- **If someone in the room says "I use it every day"**: welcome it, invite them to share a concrete example. It normalises practice and reassures hesitant participants
- **Presenter tip from the deck:** use the wave slide specifically for anyone who dismisses AI as a "new fad" — the 10-year timeline with specific product milestones is the most effective counter-narrative

### Activity #3 — AI Card Game · Maison Clarté Workshop · 45 min

**At a glance**

| | |
|---|---|
| Time | 10:30 – 11:15 (after break) |
| Format | Group workshop — 3 sequential rounds |
| Website | `atelier.html` |
| Materials | `atelier.html` open on each participant's device; one screen per group is ideal |

**Why we run it**

This is where theory becomes judgment. After Module 2 established the conceptual frame, participants now apply it to a real-world case — **Maison Clarté**, a fictional retail chain with 20 boutiques and an e-commerce site. The three rounds (Automate → Augment → Ethics) are deliberately sequenced: automation is intuitive and fast; augmentation is harder and forces the "what is uniquely human?" question; ethics is the check that prevents over-deployment.

**Key message**

> Automation is obvious. Augmentation is where the real value is — and it requires first answering the uncomfortable question: "What makes a human irreplaceable in this role?"

**Main takeaways**

- [ ] Automation and augmentation are not opposites — they are complementary levers with different risk profiles
- [ ] The augmentation question ("Can AI make this person better at what they do best?") is harder than the automation question — and more strategically important
- [ ] Every AI deployment has ethical implications; the Ethics round forces you to surface them before, not after, implementation
- [ ] The three Maison Clarté scenarios represent real situations found in most organisations

**The Maison Clarté case**

Participants meet **Julie, Head of Innovation at Maison Clarté** (lifestyle retail chain: 20 boutiques + e-commerce). Julie must identify AI solutions for three operational pain points:

| Scenario | Users | The friction |
|---|---|---|
| **A — "Les rapports que personne ne lit"** | Store managers (internal) | 20 weekly reports × ~1.5h = 30 human-hours lost/week. Reports sent to HQ, only 30% read. Real alerts (stock, HR, difficult customers) buried in noise. |
| **B — "Les vendeurs ne savent plus quoi dire"** | In-store sales staff (internal) | 2,000-SKU catalogue impossible to memorise. Staff use personal phones to Google answers. Poor intranet. No time to train. |
| **C — "Le service client sous les mêmes questions"** | Customers + staff (external + internal) | 300 messages/week. 70% are 8 recurring questions. 3 agents drowning in volume. No time for complex cases. 48h average response time vs 4h target. |

**How to run the three rounds**

**Split the room:** Form groups of 4–6 people. Each group picks one scenario (or you assign them to distribute evenly). Groups stay on the same scenario across all three rounds.

**Round 1 — Automate (8 min)**
> "Using the Automation cards on `atelier.html`, identify which repetitive, low-value tasks in your scenario could be handled by AI. For each card you select, note: which card + what expected result."

Cards available: Search & Extraction, Auto Triage & Response (Chatbot), Document Synthesis, Data Structuring, Intelligent Routing.

Facilitation tip: Groups fill this round in 5 minutes. That is expected. The speed itself is part of the lesson.

**Round 2 — Augment (8 min)**
> "Using the Augmentation cards, ask: how could AI make the people in this scenario *better* at what they do best? Think about empathy, judgment, strategy, relationship — what is uniquely human here?"

Cards available: Empathetic Writing Assistance, Recommendation Support, Preparing for Difficult Conversations, Freeing Up Human Time.

Facilitation tip: **This round always starts with a silence.** Do not fill it. The question "what makes a human irreplaceable in this role?" is not one people ask often. Let the discomfort exist. After 60–90 seconds, it will unlock a richer conversation than Round 1.

**Round 3 — Ethics (6 min)**
> "Look at the Ethics cards. Given what you decided in Rounds 1 and 2 — are there risks you haven't considered? Who could be disadvantaged? Would you implement as-is, or would you adjust?"

Cards available: Disintermediation (loss of human connection), Responsibility, Meaning at Work & Employment, Data Confidentiality & Bias.

Facilitation tip: If a participant raises "will people lose their jobs?" in Round 2, answer: "That is a legitimate question. Hold it — it is exactly what Round 3 is for."

**Plenary debrief (8 min)**

One spokesperson per group shares their top decision (30 seconds each). Then open:
- "Which round was the hardest? Why?"
- "Did any Ethics card change a decision you had already made?"
- Close: "What you just did is what most organisations skip. They jump straight to automation, skip augmentation entirely, and discover the ethical issues six months later. You just did it in 45 minutes."

**Talk-track highlights**

On the Automate/Augment distinction:

> "When you look at your work and ask 'how can AI help?', there are two very different questions. The first: can AI do this for me? That's automation — and honestly, once you understand what AI can do, the use cases are obvious. The second question is much harder: can AI make the person doing this work even better? That's augmentation. It requires first answering a question most organisations never really ask: what is the unique value of a human being in this role? Not what they do — what they *are* in that role. The empathy. The judgment in ambiguity. The intuition that a team is drifting before it shows in the numbers."

On the uncomfortable silence in Round 2:

> "The silence you'll get in the augmentation round is a good sign. It means people are genuinely thinking about what makes them human in their work. Don't rush past it."

**Tips & pitfalls**

- If a group only picks automation cards and skips augmentation entirely: prompt them with "What happens to the store manager when she no longer spends 90 minutes on the report — what does she do with that time?"
- If a group uses Ethics cards to veto everything: refocus — "Which of these risks could be mitigated by design? You don't have to choose between AI and ethics."
- The Cards Game works best when participants are on `atelier.html` on their own device — they can filter by type and flip cards individually

### Activity #4 — Lab Prompting Expert · 45 min

**At a glance**

| | |
|---|---|
| Time | 11:30 – 12:15 |
| Format | Individual hands-on (3 progressive exercises) |
| Website | `prompting.html` |
| Materials | `prompting.html` open; participants need a live ChatGPT / Claude / Gemini / Le Chat account |

**Why we run it**

This is the first time participants **directly operate an AI tool** in a structured way. The RACE method gives them a replicable framework they can take back to their daily work immediately. The three exercises are deliberately sequenced from playful (Marc's retirement party email) to professional (synthesiser, reflection assistant) — this reduces the intimidation of a first prompt and builds confidence progressively.

**Key message**

> Think of AI as an extremely capable junior who just joined the organisation. They know nothing about your context, your constraints, or your audience. The more precisely you brief them — with a role, a task, context, and requirements — the better the output.

**Main takeaways**

- [ ] AI is a reasoning engine, not a search engine — you get out what you put in
- [ ] The RACE framework (Rôle, Action, Contexte, Exigences) produces reliably better results than unstructured requests
- [ ] Iteration is the rule — the first result is a starting point, not a final answer
- [ ] Never paste sensitive data (passwords, personal HR data, confidential financials) into a consumer AI tool

**The RACE framework**

| Letter | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **R — Rôle** | Who should the AI be? | "Act as an experienced project manager at [Company]" |
| **A — Action** | What exactly should it do? | "Write an invitation email", "Summarise this text" |
| **C — Contexte** | For whom and why? | "For Marc's retirement party — 15 years at the company, cycling and cheese enthusiast, Friday 5pm in the auditorium" |
| **E — Exigences** | Format, tone, constraints? | "Humorous tone, max 3 paragraphs, include a call to action for an online collection" |

> **Note for facilitators:** The deck uses **RTCF** (Rôle, Tâche, Contexte, Format). The training website and the live RACE Builder use **RACE** (Rôle, Action, Contexte, Exigences). Use **RACE** throughout — it matches the tool participants will use on screen.

**How to run the three exercises**

**Exercise 1 — Free prompt (5 min)**
> "Open your AI tool of choice. Ask it to write an invitation email for your colleague Marc's retirement party. Write the prompt however feels natural to you. No guidance — just try."

Debrief (5 min): Ask 2–3 people to share their result. What was good? What was missing? Establish the "before" state.

**Exercise 2 — Apply RACE (5 min)**
> "Now rewrite the same request using the RACE framework on `prompting.html`. Use the RACE Builder: fill in Role, Action, Context, Requirements, click 'Copy Prompt', paste it into your AI tool."

RACE example to show:
- R: Act as an enthusiastic colleague
- A: Write an invitation email for Marc's retirement party
- C: Marc is retiring after 15 years; he is a passionate cycling and cheese fan; the party is Friday at 5pm in the office auditorium
- E: Humorous tone; maximum 3 paragraphs; include a call to action for his online collection

Debrief (2 min): Compare the two outputs. What changed? What does this tell you about how AI processes instructions?

**Exercise 3 — Apply RACE to a professional case (5 min + 5 min debrief)**
Participants choose one of two cases available on `prompting.html`:

- **Case A — "Le Synthétiseur":** Take a dense document (an internal regulation, a research article). Ask AI to simplify it for frontline staff and extract 3 concrete action items.
- **Case B — "L'Assistant de Réflexion":** Prepare a difficult conversation, launch a new project, or find team animation ideas. Key instruction: tell AI to "ask me the right questions first before giving answers."

**The three reflexes to reinforce at the end**

Before closing this activity, name these three out loud:

1. **Critical mind:** AI can hallucinate — always re-read before publishing or acting
2. **Data hygiene:** Never paste sensitive data into a consumer AI tool (use an enterprise-grade version if available)
3. **Iteration:** The first output is a draft. The real value comes from the conversation that follows.

**Talk-track highlights**

Opening the activity:

> "A prompt is not a search query. When you Google something, you're looking for an existing document. When you prompt AI, you're commissioning something new. The quality of the commission determines the quality of the result. Think of AI as a brilliant junior who just walked in the door. They're fast, they're capable, they have no idea how your organisation works, and they will do *exactly* what you tell them to do — no more, no less. If your brief is vague, the output will be vague."

On iteration:

> "The golden rule: iteration. You almost never get the perfect result on the first try. AI is conversational — dialogue with it to refine the output. The RACE framework minimises the number of iterations needed, but never eliminates them."

**Tips & pitfalls**

- Some participants will not have an AI tool account — pair them with someone who does for Exercise 1; they can use the `prompting.html` RACE Builder independently for Exercises 2 and 3
- The Marc email exercise always produces funny outputs — lean into it, laughter at this point releases tension and builds confidence
- The "reflection assistant" case (Case B) is often the most valuable for senior participants — it shifts AI from a writing tool to a thinking partner
- Watch for participants who skip the RACE structure and just write a long paragraph — gently redirect them to the four-field structure

### Activity #5 — Mon Premier Gem · 45 min

**At a glance**

| | |
|---|---|
| Time | 13:15 – 14:00 |
| Format | Individual hands-on |
| Website | **No page on the training website yet** — participants open **gemini.google.com** directly |
| Materials | Participants need a Google account; facilitator needs a demo Gem prepared in advance |

> **Site note:** This activity is in the deck but does not yet have a dedicated page on the training website. Direct participants to gemini.google.com. A `gem.html` page is planned for a future update.

**Why we run it**

Lab Prompting taught participants to write better one-off prompts. This activity takes the next step: **a configured AI assistant that remembers its mission across every conversation**. The Gem is the day's tangible take-away — participants leave with something they built themselves, configured for a recurring task in their actual life.

**Key message**

> The shift from "writing a prompt every time" to "having an AI assistant that already knows its mission" is the shift from AI as a tool to AI as a collaborator.

**Main takeaways**

- [ ] A Gem is a persistent AI assistant within Gemini, configured with a specific mission
- [ ] Building a Gem requires the same thinking as a RACE prompt — just saved permanently
- [ ] A well-configured Gem eliminates the need to rewrite context at the start of every conversation
- [ ] The right use case for a Gem is any task you perform repeatedly (weekly report synthesis, email drafting, meeting preparation…)

**What is a Gem?**

A Gem is a customised, specialised version of Gemini for a recurring task. It has:

- **Context memory:** it remembers the "mission" you gave it for every new conversation
- **Speed:** no need to write a long prompt each time — it is ready to use
- Three illustrative examples from the deck:
  - *Le Coach d'écriture* — criticises your writing style, corrects errors, helps you find the right word
  - *Le Movie Matchmaker* — knows your two favourite films and your streaming services; finds the perfect match in your current catalogue
  - *Bob le Bricoleur* — photograph a leak or a flat-pack; it identifies the tools, estimates the time, generates a step-by-step tutorial

**How to run the activity**

1. **Open discussion (3 min):** "What is a Gem on Gemini?" — take 2–3 answers from the room before explaining
2. **Demo your pre-built Gem (4 min):** Show a Gem you built before the session. Demonstrate that opening a new conversation with it requires no context-setting — it already knows its role
3. **Participants build their Gem (10 min):**
   - Go to gemini.google.com → "My Gems" → "New Gem"
   - Give it a name and a description
   - Write the instructions (this is a saved RACE prompt — role, recurring action, standing context, output requirements)
4. **Test and iterate (10 min):** Each participant opens a new conversation with their Gem and runs a real task through it
5. **Debrief (8 min):** 2–3 participants share their Gem concept and what they discovered during testing

**Suggested Gem concepts to offer if participants are stuck**

| Gem name | Use case | Standing instruction |
|---|---|---|
| Mon Assistant Réunion | Prepare a weekly team meeting | "Act as a meeting facilitator. When I give you the agenda topics, ask me clarifying questions, then generate a structured agenda with time blocks and expected outcomes" |
| Mon Synthétiseur | Condense long documents | "Act as an expert editor. When I paste a text, extract the 3 key ideas, 3 action items, and 1 open question. Output in bullet form, max 150 words." |
| Mon Coach Mail | Draft professional emails | "Act as a senior communications advisor. When I describe a situation and recipient, draft a professional email in the appropriate tone. Always offer two versions: formal and conversational." |

**Tips & pitfalls**

- Participants sometimes over-engineer their first Gem — encourage simplicity: "Start with one task you do every week"
- Some participants will not have a Google account — pair them or have them observe a neighbour's build
- The Gem instructions are essentially a saved RACE prompt — explicitly connect back to Activity #4 to reinforce the framework
- If time runs short, the "test" phase can be abbreviated — the important outcome is that the Gem exists and has been named

### Activity #6 — L'Agentique en Action · 30 min

**At a glance**

| | |
|---|---|
| Time | 14:15 – 14:45 |
| Format | Live demo + plenary discussion |
| Website | `agentique.html` (for exploration after the demo) |
| Materials | Salesforce Agentforce demo environment (prepared in advance); `agentique.html` on screen |

**Why we run it**

After spending the morning understanding AI concepts and the afternoon getting hands-on with Generative AI tools, participants now see **where the technology is heading**. The Agentforce demo shows the qualitative leap from a prompt-based copilot to an autonomous enterprise agent — one that handles 80% of a B2C customer service flow without human intervention. This is the day's horizon activity: it expands what participants believe is possible.

**Key message**

> An AI Agent is not just a faster chatbot. It reasons, acts, remembers, and operates on trusted business data — autonomously, at scale, and with humans staying at the helm for what matters most.

**Main takeaways**

- [ ] The Chatbot → Copilot → Agent progression is not cosmetic — each step increases the level of autonomous action
- [ ] Agentforce can handle 80% of routine customer service cases with zero human intervention — freeing agents for the 20% that genuinely needs them
- [ ] The three pillars of a responsible Agentic Enterprise: Ethics by Design, Human at the Helm, Efficient Resource Use
- [ ] Humans remain in control of strategy, high-stakes decisions, and anything that requires judgment, empathy, or accountability

**Demo script (Agentforce B2C E-Commerce)**

Demonstrate a live Agentforce agent handling a customer returns request:

1. Customer initiates contact → agent reads the order history from Salesforce data
2. Agent verifies return eligibility against policy
3. Agent proposes options (exchange, refund, store credit) and processes the customer's choice
4. Agent logs the resolution and closes the case — zero human intervention

Then show the **Agentforce Command Center** — the interface where human supervisors monitor agent activity, review escalations, and maintain oversight.

**The Chatbot / Copilot / Agent comparator (use `agentique.html`)**

After the demo, direct participants to `agentique.html` and ask them to click through the three tabs:

| Tab | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Chatbot** | Fixed rules, repetitive tasks | Password reset, FAQ |
| **Copilot** | Smart, tailored assistance | "Einstein, what is the status of this order?" |
| **Agent** | Autonomous — learns, acts, trusts data | Handles return end-to-end; zero human touch for 80% of cases |

**The Human vs Agent allocation (use `agentique.html`)**

This section always generates discussion. Walk through it before opening the floor:

| What humans keep | What agents do well |
|---|---|
| Judgment in ambiguity | Fast, fatigue-free execution |
| Empathy and active listening | Pattern identification |
| Accountability | Massive data processing |
| Expression of values | 24/7 availability |
| Strategic intuition | Consistent responses |
| Cross-domain knowledge | Workflow automation |
| Trust relationships | Multi-task simultaneously |

Ask the room: "Looking at your own role — which tasks from the right column are you currently spending time on? What would you do with that time back?"

**The three pillars of a responsible Agentic Enterprise**

| Pillar | What it means | Salesforce commitment |
|---|---|---|
| **Ethics by Design** | 5 Trusted AI principles embedded in product: Human Rights, Privacy, Safety, Transparency, Inclusion | Salesforce Office of Ethical & Humane Use |
| **Human at the Helm** | Humans review and validate on critical decisions; Agentforce Command Center for oversight | 80% of customers say human validation is essential |
| **Efficient Resource Use** | Choose the most energy-efficient LLM; select lowest-carbon hosting; preset efficient prompt guardrails | AI Energy Score, Sustainability Outlook Report |

**Tips & pitfalls**

- Test the Agentforce demo environment the morning of the training — live demos carry technical risk
- Have a backup: a recorded demo video in case the live environment is unavailable
- The "Human at the Helm" pillar is the most important for participants who expressed concerns about job displacement in Activity #3 — connect back explicitly: "In the Card Game this morning, some of you flagged the responsibility question. This is the structural answer to that question."
- Leave 5 minutes at the end for `agentique.html` self-exploration — the resource cards link to the Einstein Trust Layer, Trusted AI principles, and Agentforce documentation

### Activity #7 — Bilan Profil IA & Conclusion · 30 min

**At a glance**

| | |
|---|---|
| Time | 14:45 – 15:15 |
| Format | Individual survey + group reflection + facilitator closing |
| Website | `home.html` → Bilan button |
| Materials | Spider Graph template (same as Activity #1) |

**Why we run it**

This activity **closes the loop** opened at 09:00. Participants re-run the Spider Graph and compare it against their morning baseline. Even after one day, the scores shift — particularly on Maîtrise (they have written structured prompts) and Vigilance (they have heard about hallucination). The comparison is the proof of learning, and it gives each participant a personalised take-away: "here is specifically where I grew today, and here is where I want to go next."

**Key message**

> AI literacy is not a destination — it is a practice. Today was day one. The Spider Graph shows you how far you can move in eight hours; the resources on the site show you how to keep moving.

**Main takeaways**

- [ ] I can see concrete progress between my morning and evening Spider Graph scores
- [ ] I have a personal next step — one thing I will try differently in my work next week
- [ ] I know where to go to keep learning (module resource cards, Salesforce Trusted AI, Agentforce docs)

**How to facilitate**

1. Ask everyone to return to `home.html` and click the Bilan button (2 min)
2. While they fill in the form, redisplay the Spider Graph template
3. Once forms are in, ask the same show-of-hands questions as the morning — sketch the evening aggregate next to the morning one (5 min)
4. Ask: "What moved for you today? What surprised you about the shift?" (10 min open discussion)
5. Ask each participant (or tables, if large group) to name: "One thing I'm doing differently next week because of today" (5 min)
6. Facilitator closing (5 min) — see talk-track below

**Talk-track — Closing**

> "Look at where you started this morning and where you are now. Some of you told me at 09:00 that you'd never really used AI at work. You just spent a day understanding the technology, applying a prompting framework that professional engineers use, and building a personal AI assistant. That's not nothing. The Spider Graph doesn't lie — you moved.
>
> AI is not a tool you learn once. It's a practice you develop over time — like managing people, or running meetings. The important thing is not to wait until you understand it perfectly before you start using it. Start with one real task. Use RACE. Iterate. And come back to the training site whenever you need to revisit a concept or a framework.
>
> Thank you for the energy you brought today."

**Tips & pitfalls**

- If time is short, the individual comparison can be done mentally rather than formally — ask "on which dimension did you move most?" rather than redrawing the graph
- Some participants will feel their scores "should" be higher — reassure them that the Spider Graph is a tool for direction, not a performance metric
- The "one thing next week" commitment is powerful — write them on a board or shared doc if possible, so participants feel accountable to each other

## 7. Appendix

### A. Key glossary (for facilitator reference)

| Term | Plain-English definition |
|---|---|
| **LLM** | Large Language Model — the statistical engine behind tools like ChatGPT and Claude; trained on billions of documents |
| **Prompt** | The instruction you give to an AI — the quality of the input directly determines the quality of the output |
| **Hallucination** | When an AI generates something that looks correct but is factually wrong — it predicts plausibly, not truthfully |
| **Token** | The unit of text AI processes — roughly 0.75 words; LLMs have a "context window" limit of how many tokens they can handle at once |
| **Copilot** | An AI assistant embedded in a workflow (e.g. Microsoft Copilot in Word, Einstein GPT in Salesforce) — helps, but doesn't act autonomously |
| **Agent IA** | An AI that receives a goal and chains autonomous actions to achieve it — browse, write, send, book, decide |
| **RAG** | Retrieval-Augmented Generation — a technique that connects an LLM to a specific knowledge base so it answers using your data, not its generic training |
| **Fine-tuning** | Retraining a base LLM on a specific dataset to specialise its behaviour for a particular domain |
| **Biais algorithmique** | When AI perpetuates or amplifies the biases present in its training data — subtle, systematic, and easy to miss |
| **Einstein Trust Layer** | Salesforce's data security architecture that ensures your business data is never used to train external LLMs |
| **Prompt injection** | A security attack that tries to hijack an AI agent's behaviour by embedding hidden instructions in content it processes |
| **Gem** | A saved, persistent AI assistant in Gemini — configured with a standing mission so it needs no re-briefing per conversation |
| **Agentforce** | Salesforce's agentic AI platform — builds, deploys, and monitors AI agents that act on enterprise data |
